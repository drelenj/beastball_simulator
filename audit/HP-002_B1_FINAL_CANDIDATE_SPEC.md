## 2. Principles
- **P1** Physical resolution is a deterministic function of physical state, commands, environment and modelled randomness from named seeded streams.
- **P2** Cognition (generation, evaluation, selection) reads observable, known and inferred information only. Motor Control (MC) reads the athlete's own body, physical constraints and the command given. Neither reads a hidden opponent attribute. Physical resolution (PHY) uses true state, including both athletes' true ratings.
- **P3** Belief updates within a match are allowed; no rating development is authorized.
- **P4** Matching B0 or T3 proves historical physical preservation, not canonical correctness.

## 3. Motor Control boundary (clarification 1)
MC **may**: compute execution geometry for a destination or target it was given, from the cognitive intention and the athlete's beliefs: path and braking profile, intercept timing along a believed trajectory, ballistic aim solution to a chosen target, steering and take-off timing, release error from ratings. MC **may not**: choose or improve a tactical destination, select among targets, re-pick a receiving spot, pick an arc by weighing opponents or obstacles, or substitute a different destination when the given one is poor. If the command is infeasible MC returns infeasibility to cognition; it does not decide an alternative.
Rule of thumb: *MC output = f(command, belief of the object to be met, own body and limits).* Other players, the goal, rules and tactics are not inputs to MC geometry. Obstacle-aware choices (for example the Archer's high-versus-low arc, which today weighs blockers) belong to cognition; MC solves the arc it is given. Interception along a believed ball path is execution geometry only while it is a deterministic function of that path and the athlete's limits.
Tests: **MF-1** field-read check (MC reads only P2 inputs); **MF-4** endpoint fidelity (MC endpoint equals the cognitive endpoint within feasibility tolerance, or an infeasibility report); **MF-5** perturbation (changing other players' positions, with the command and belief fixed, does not change MC output).

## 7. Information-quality sensitivity testing (clarification 4; added to Gate C)
Purpose: show the pipeline responds coherently to information quality, with no assumption about how large the effect should be. Protocol: a test-only harness (not a game control; ratings remain the only knobs) scales perception parameters (observation noise, sensory latency, memory, detection probability, field of view) one at a time over a grid, with common random numbers and fixed rosters and seeds. Checks: **IQ-1** limit identities (at zero noise and latency, belief equals truth to numerical precision; this is a perception setting, not hidden-state access); **IQ-2** at every quality level the hide-only perturbation of hidden state changes nothing (isolation does not depend on quality); **IQ-3** determinism and valid states at degraded limits; **IQ-4** dose-response curves for pre-registered metrics (decision-change rate, pass completion, interception timing, Archer hit rate, Keeper saves), reported with no pass/fail on slope or size. Metrics and tolerances follow O5.

## 10a. Rules statements received, referred to the rules audit (R2)
Recorded as stated by DreLen (2026-11-07); I have not changed any rule or code. B0 remains as it is.
1. **Archer legal range:** any spot outside the Keeper Zones. *B0 differs:* the engine hard-codes a 10–70 m distance window for arrow legality and hits (`engine.py:1034, 1056`) and for the Archer's option filter (`match.py:988, 1008`), and the structure policy also restricts targets to players within 9 m of the carrier (`match.py:988`). Under the stated canon all three are rule discrepancies for the B1 rules baseline. Physical reach is still limited by arrow speed and drag; when no ballistic solution exists, Motor Control reports infeasibility to cognition (§3).
2. **Archer targets:** all opposing field players while the opposing team has possession (possession persists through loose and airborne ball); Keepers are not field players.
3. **Keeper immunity:** immunities hold only inside the Keeper's own Keeper Zone, where the Keeper may stay indefinitely; they are lost on leaving it. *B0 appears consistent for arrows* (a Keeper in its own zone is not a legal target, `engine.py:1032`); other immunities not checked.
4. **Keeper on offense:** cannot be contacted, so may pass freely out of the zone; is not eligible to score except own goals. *Not yet checked against B0* (the engine has an own-goal scoring path, `engine.py:1149`; Keeper scoring eligibility not verified).
5. **Contact protection is exclusive to the Keeper (DreLen, 2026-11-07 20:38).** Not extended to other players who gain possession. Rationale recorded: a protected carrier would undermine defending and invite timer exploits; the first-tackle-as-count rule (Codex T2 §8.1, v0.3 only) already softens immediate tackles on other players.
6. **Time limit on the ball in the possessing team's own Keeper Zone (DreLen, 2026-11-07 20:58): counts regardless of who holds the ball.** **Limit: 5 consecutive seconds (DreLen, 2026-11-07 21:42).** **Consequence of exceeding it: turnover (DreLen, 2026-11-07 23:45); I am applying the same center-line opening contact scrimmage as item 7 unless told otherwise.** Findings behind it (source-derived, not tested): B0 limits only the *Keeper carrying* the ball (`keeper_hold_ms >= 5000` is an `illegal 'keeper_hold'`, `engine.py:1654, 1714`); that counter resets on any non-forced release (`engine.py:355`) and after 3 s outside the zone (`engine.py:1657`); B0's legacy controller makes the Keeper distribute after 0.4 s (`match.py:949`), so parking never arises in B0 play, but would be reachable in B1 once that authored prescription is removed. The new rule must therefore count ball time in the zone, not Keeper time.
7. **Zone-return violation (DreLen, 2026-11-08 08:35): rule text, adapted from the NBA backcourt rule.** *"A player shall not be the first to touch a ball which he or a teammate caused to go from outside the keeper zone to inside the keeper zone while his team was in control of the ball."* Consequence (DreLen, 2026-11-07 21:42): turnover; ball spotted at the center line for an opening contact scrimmage. This replaces my earlier paraphrase and subsumes both of its clauses (a possession that started outside, and one that started inside, left, and came back, are both an outside-to-inside crossing). Differences from the earlier paraphrase, as I read the text: the violation is the offense's *first touch* of the ball in the zone, not the entry itself; a defender who touches it first removes the violation; a ball the offense did not cause to enter (defender deflection) is unrestricted; "in control" excludes a shot or loose ball. Not in B0.
   **Definitions (DreLen, 2026-11-08 08:49):** (i) a ball counts as inside the zone by where it is first touched or lands, as in the NBA rule (I read "yes" as choosing that over ground-position projection; tell me if you meant the other); (ii) "in control" excludes a shot or a loose ball such as a fumble; (iii) a carrier crossing into the zone with the ball is a violation at the moment he crosses.
Implications recorded for the rules audit and B1 design (my analysis, not decisions): (a) it is a legality constraint on carry and pass options, so cognition needs public rules state: where the current possession started and whether it has left the zone; both are observable to every athlete; the engine adjudicates; (b) back-passes to a Keeper in the zone become illegal in most situations, which removes a B0 tactic and changes Keeper usage; (c) the rule needs public, attributable touch events (who touched first, whether the offense caused the entry) and a definition of when the ball counts as "inside" while airborne (NBA uses where the ball is first touched or lands; open), and of "in control" (open); (c2) because grasp is automatic physical resolution (§4), an offensive athlete cannot choose to "not catch" a ball that reaches his hands; avoidance lives in cognitive catch preparation (positioning, not going for the ball), so a violation can arise from unavoidable contact, and a pass into the zone has little value to the offense, which the evaluator must learn from the public rule; (d) the ownership split holds: the rule is a RULE, adjudicated by PHY/rules; Motor Control never reasons about it; (e) it is a canon change to B1's rules baseline, not an edit to B0; new tests RL-3 (zone-return scenarios: possession starting inside/outside, leaving and re-entering, defender deflection, Keeper catch inside versus outside the zone, loose ball) and an information check that athletes' options respect the public flag.
Open for the rules audit: which engine implementation provides the "opening contact scrimmage" restart (the contact scrimmage belongs to `RULES_V03`, which is not enabled in B0; `restarts_v1` has an older path); the "inside" and "in control" definitions above; and how items 3 and 4 interact (immunity lost outside the zone versus uncontactable on offense), and whether an out-of-zone Keeper on defense is contactable.
